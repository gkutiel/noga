Accurate electricity demand forecasting is critical for market participants in
deregulated power markets, where procurement decisions must be made before
actual demand is observed.  
Traditional forecasting approaches minimize symmetric
loss functions such as mean squared error (MSE), which implicitly treat over- and
under-prediction as equally costly. 

In practice the costs depends on many factors, 
such as the availability of reserve capacity, the availability of renewable energies, 
the cost of procuring additional electricity, and the penalties for failing to meet demand.


A better approach is to fully characterize the uncertainty and distribution of the actual demand.
A richer description will allow market participants to make better procurement decisions, and to reduce the expected cost.
It will also allow them to quantify the risk of failing to meet demand, and to make more informed decisions about how much reserve capacity to procure.

In this project we propose to develop a new approach for electricity demand forecasting. 
Instead of output a single point estimate, we will output a full predictive distribution of the actual demand.
Furthermore, our model will also output a distribution of the renewable energy generation, 
and will integrate these distributions to provide a comprehensive probabilistic forecast for electricity supply and demand.
