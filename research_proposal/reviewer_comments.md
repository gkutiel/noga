## 4. Page 4 — §1.1 Background and Motivation (Sticky Note)

## 10. Page 11 — §3.4 Integration into a Net-Demand Distribution (Highlight)
- **Highlighted text:** "(heat raises cooling demand and PV output simultaneously"
- **Comment:** This is incorrect. Heat raises cooling demand, but the PV production is not
  raised by the heat but rather by the longer daytime hours and the higher number of clear
  sky hours. The normal direct radiation at peak time is almost similar during the entire
  year, but what changes is the solar incidence angle which determines the effective
  incoming global radiation that control production, especially where the panels are
  stationary and do not follow the sun path in the sky. Additionally, as I wrote above,
  higher air temperatures reduce the panels' efficiency as long as they are not actively
  cooled (for example, by running cool water in pipes attached to the panels, which I think
  is not prevalent in Israel). This makes the precise prediction of solar production more
  complex, even without considering the decentralized nature of panel geographic
  distribution.
- **Status:** Addressed (§3.4 corrected: PV output rises from long, clear days — extended
  daylight and few clouds — not from heat, which slightly lowers panel efficiency; the
  demand–PV dependence is now attributed to shared weather/seasonal drivers).
