The objective of this study was to determine whether a single demand forecaster
that receives day-ahead prices as inputs and is trained end-to-end on the
realised procurement cost (Section~\ref{sec:modeling}) can outperform models
trained to minimise MSE, models trained with a fixed cost asymmetry, post-hoc
cost calibrations of both, and the real operational forecast of the Israeli
system operator.  This section describes the dataset
(Section~\ref{sec:dataset}), the neural architecture and the training
objectives we compare, the post-hoc calibration baselines, and the protocol
under which they are evaluated (Section~\ref{sec:model-training}).

\subsection{Model Training}
\label{sec:model-training}

All model variants share the same architecture: the prediction is expressed as
a \emph{soft attention} over a short window of recent historical demand values
plus a learned additive correction.  Weather conditions (air temperature, wet-bulb
temperature, and relative humidity --- all raw fields reported directly by the
IMS --- at three meteorological stations),
temporal patterns (time of day, day of week, month of year) and, for the
price-aware variants, the known prices jointly determine both the attention
weights and the correction term.  Temporal features are encoded via learned
embeddings; weather and price features enter as continuous inputs.  The model
operates at 5-minute granularity and produces a day-ahead forecast.  The
variants differ \emph{only} in their training objective and in whether they see
prices, so that any difference in cost can be attributed to these two factors.

\subsubsection{Data Splitting}

The dataset is partitioned chronologically:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Training set:} April~2023 -- August~2024.
    \item \textbf{Calibration set:} September -- December~2024.  Used for early
          stopping and for fitting every post-hoc calibrator.
    \item \textbf{Test set:} January -- December~2025, held out for final
          evaluation.
\end{itemize}

The chronological split ensures that no future information leaks into training
or calibration.  In particular, calibrators are fitted on predictions for data
that the model was not trained on, so that their residuals are representative of
test-time errors.

\subsubsection{Neural Network Architecture}

For each target 5-minute slot $t$, the model receives the following inputs:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Historical demand.}  A window of $H = 10$ consecutive
          5-minute demand readings $\mathbf{h} = (h_1,\ldots,h_H)$ ending
          exactly 24~hours before $t$ (i.e.\ the same time-of-day window
          from the previous day).

    \item \textbf{Weather features.}  Nine continuous variables measured at
          time $t$: air temperature, wet-bulb temperature, and relative humidity
          for three stations (Haifa, Jerusalem, Tel~Aviv).

    \item \textbf{Price features} (price-aware variants only).  The log
          day-ahead price $\log\pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t$ and the over-prediction
          price ratio $\pi^o_t/\pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t$, both known at decision
          time.  The under-prediction price $\pi^u_t$ is never an input.

    \item \textbf{Temporal embeddings.}  Learned embeddings for time of day
          (288 positions, dimension~16), day of week (7~positions,
          dimension~3), and month of year (12~positions, dimension~2).
\end{itemize}

These are concatenated and passed through a small MLP with two hidden layers of
width~32 and LeakyReLU activations, producing an output vector
$\mathbf{o} \in \mathbb{R}^{H+1}$.
The first $H$ outputs are treated as attention logits over the history window;
the last output is an additive correction scalar~$\delta$.  The forecast is:

\begin{equation}
    \hat{y} \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{H} \mathrm{softmax}(\mathbf{o}_{1:H})_k\,h_k
             \;+\; \delta,
    \label{eq:forecast}
\end{equation}

where demand values and prices are normalised by a constant scale factor of
$100$ during training and rescaled at inference time.  All models are trained
with the Adam optimiser~\parencite{kingma2014adam} at a learning rate
$2\times10^{-4}$, batch size~10\,000, and up to 500 epochs with early stopping
on the calibration set (patience 20, evaluated every 10~epochs).

\subsubsection{Training Objectives}

We compare the following training objectives:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MSE} (L2) and \textbf{L1} --- symmetric baselines targeting
          the conditional mean and median.
    \item \textbf{Pinball $r\!:\!1$} for $r\in\{5,10,20\}$ --- fixed-ratio
          asymmetric baselines targeting the constant quantile $r/(r+1)$.
    \item \textbf{Price-aware cost (proposed)} --- price features as inputs and
          the mean excess cost (Equation~\eqref{eq:excess}) with the
          \emph{realised} prices as the loss.
\end{itemize}

Two ablations separate the effect of the price inputs from that of the
price-weighted loss: \textbf{cost without price inputs}, trained on the same
loss but without seeing prices, and \textbf{MSE with price inputs}, which sees
prices but is trained with a symmetric loss.  Because the loss is
differentiable almost everywhere, the price-aware objective adds no
computational overhead compared to L1 training.

\subsubsection{Post-Hoc Cost Calibration}
\label{sec:posthoc}

To test whether the benefit of the unified model could be obtained more
cheaply, we apply four post-hoc calibrations to the outputs of the MSE, L1,
pinball and Noga forecasts.  All of them are fitted on the calibration set and
use only information available at decision time.  Several of them require an
estimate of the cost-optimal quantile; we estimate
$\hat\tau_t$ by plugging into Equation~\eqref{eq:optimal_threshold} a binned
estimate of $\mathbb{E}[\pi^u_t/\pi^o_t \mid \pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t]$ fitted on
past prices (training and calibration periods).

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{C1 --- global affine.}  $g(\hat y)=a\hat y + b$, with $(a,b)$
          minimising the mean excess cost on the calibration set.  This is the
          calibration used in earlier work and in the previous version of this
          study.
    \item \textbf{C2 --- price-aware newsvendor (predict-then-optimise).}
          $g(\hat y_t) = \hat y_t + Q_{h(t)}(\hat\tau_t)$, where $Q_h$ is the
          empirical quantile function of the calibration residuals
          $y-\hat y$ in hour-of-day $h$.  This is the textbook two-stage
          approach: first forecast, then shift the forecast to the
          price-dependent cost-optimal quantile.
    \item \textbf{C3 --- learned residual.}
          $g(\hat y_t) = \hat y_t + \mathbf{w}^\top\phi_t$, where $\phi_t$
          contains $\hat y_t$, the price features, $\hat\tau_t$ and an
          hour-of-day one-hot encoding, and $\mathbf{w}$ minimises the
          excess cost on the calibration set.
    \item \textbf{C4 --- quantile bank.}  Applicable to the fixed-ratio
          models only: given the L1, Pinball 5:1, 10:1 and 20:1 models,
          which target $\tau\in\{0.5, 0.833, 0.909, 0.952\}$, each slot
          receives the linear interpolation between the two models whose
          quantiles bracket $\hat\tau_t$.
\end{itemize}

C2--C4 are price-aware: they use the same known prices as the proposed model.
They differ from it in that the price information enters \emph{after} a model
has been trained on a price-blind objective, so they can only shift the
forecast according to the price and the hour, not according to how price
interacts with the weather, the calendar and the recent demand.

\subsubsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval-protocol}

The primary metric is the \textbf{excess cost} (Equation~\eqref{eq:excess})
on the test year, in \textsc{nis} per year, i.e.\ the cost above that of a
perfect forecast.  We also report the MAE, the RMSE and the empirical coverage
$P(y_t\le p_t)$.  Each configuration is repeated for three training seeds and
three independent draws of the synthetic imbalance prices; we report the mean
and standard deviation over these nine runs.  Statistical significance of the
difference in daily excess cost between the proposed model and each baseline is
assessed with the Diebold--Mariano test~\parencite{diebold1995comparing} with a
Newey--West variance estimate (lag~7 days).
