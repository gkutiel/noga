When forecast errors are settled at market prices, the cost-optimal demand
forecast is a quantile that moves with the price.  We proposed the first
unified model that forecasts demand and prices from one shared representation
and trains the demand forecast on the price-weighted procurement cost.  On a
new open dataset of the Israeli market it beats demand-only models trained for
accuracy or with a fixed asymmetry, a joint demand-and-price network trained
for accuracy, post-hoc corrections of all of them, and the operator's own
forecast---because its safety margin rises with the price.  It would save
about \$27 million per year in Israel against the published operator
forecast, roughly a billion dollars per year at US scale, and it does so with
fewer than 2{,}000 parameters trained in five minutes on a laptop.

\paragraph*{Limitations.}  (i)~The imbalance prices are synthetic, anchored on
the real day-ahead price; the savings depend on their level.  (ii)~All
results use one draw of these prices.  (iii)~The post-hoc corrections are
fitted on training-set forecasts, whose errors are smaller than on the test
set, so their margins may be slightly too small.  (iv)~We assume a price
taker and ignore the effect of the forecast on the prices.  (v)~The model
produces a point forecast, not a full predictive distribution.

\paragraph*{Future work.}  Real imbalance prices, from the operator or market
participants, would turn the savings estimate into a measurement.  Further
directions are a scarcity variant of the price generator in which shortfalls are most expensive when
the whole system is short, richer price inputs (fuel prices, renewable
forecasts), distributional forecasts trained on price-weighted objectives,
and applying the unified model to larger markets.
