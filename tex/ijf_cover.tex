\begin{letter}{The Editor-in-Chief\\International Journal of Forecasting}

\opening{Dear Editor,}

We submit the manuscript ``A Unified Price-and-Demand Model for Cost-Optimal
Day-Ahead Electricity Demand Forecasting: Evidence from the Israeli Market''
for consideration as a research article in the \emph{International Journal of
Forecasting}.

When day-ahead forecast errors are settled at market prices, the forecast that
minimises cost is not the expected demand but a quantile that moves with the
price.  Demand and prices are nevertheless forecast separately and for
accuracy, and cost-oriented load forecasting uses a fixed cost ratio.  The
paper proposes, to our knowledge, the first model that forecasts demand and
prices from one shared representation and trains the demand forecast on the
price-weighted procurement cost.  The paper's main contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
    \item A unified model whose safety margin rises with the price, as
          newsvendor theory requires.  It is significantly cheaper
          (Diebold--Mariano tests) than demand-only models trained for
          accuracy or with a fixed asymmetry, than a joint demand-and-price
          network trained for accuracy and corrected afterwards, and than the
          system operator's real forecast after the best correction.
    \item A savings estimate in money: about \$27 million per year against the
          Israeli operator's published forecast, roughly \$1 billion per year
          at US scale.
    \item A new open dataset of the Israeli market that aligns 5-minute
          national demand, the operator's real day-ahead forecast, real
          day-ahead prices and weather, together with the complete code.
\end{itemize}

We believe the paper suits the journal because it treats forecasting as an
input to a decision and evaluates forecasts by the cost of that decision,
alongside conventional accuracy measures.  In line with the journal's
reproducibility policy, all data and code are provided as supplementary
material and reproduce every table and figure.  The model has fewer than
2{,}000 parameters and trains in about five minutes on a standard laptop.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under
consideration elsewhere.  All authors have approved the submission and
declare no competing interests.

\closing{Sincerely,}
