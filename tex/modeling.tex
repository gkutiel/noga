\subsection{Procurement Cost}

A market participant commits to a quantity $p_t$ for every slot $t$ of the
next day before observing the demand $y_t$.  Let $\pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t$ be the
day-ahead price, $\pi^u_t$ the price of each unit of shortfall and $\pi^o_t$
the value lost on each unit of surplus, with
\begin{equation}
    \pi^{u}_t \;>\; \pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t \;>\; \pi^{o}_t \;>\; 0.
    \label{eq:price-order}
\end{equation}
The realised cost is
$C_t = \pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t \min(y_t, p_t) + \pi^{u}_t (y_t - p_t)^{+} +
\pi^{o}_t (p_t - y_t)^{+}$.  The consumed energy $\pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t y_t$ is
paid whatever the forecast, so we work with the \emph{excess cost} above a
perfect forecast,
\begin{equation}
    E_t(p_t, y_t) = C_t - \pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t y_t
    = \underbrace{(\pi^{u}_t - \pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t)}_{c^{u}_t}\,(y_t - p_t)^{+}
    + \underbrace{\pi^{o}_t}_{c^{o}_t}\,(p_t - y_t)^{+},
    \label{eq:excess}
\end{equation}
a pinball loss whose two slopes are \emph{prices} that change from slot to
slot.  We consider the uncertainty of demand only; production-side
uncertainty, e.g.\ from renewables~\parencite{morales2014integrating}, is
outside our scope.

\paragraph*{Information.}  The day-ahead price is set by the market
\emph{after} the purchase decision, so none of the slot's prices
$\pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t,\pi^u_t,\pi^o_t$ is known when $p_t$ is chosen.  The
forecaster sees only what is available a day ahead: demand and prices up to
24~hours before $t$, the weather forecast and the calendar.  Historical prices
are known after the fact and can be used for training.

\subsection{The Optimal Forecast Is a Price-Dependent Quantile}
\label{sec:newsvendor}

For known prices, minimising Equation~\eqref{eq:excess} is the newsvendor
problem~\parencite{arrow1951optimal}, whose solution is the critical fractile
\begin{equation}
    p^*_t = F_t^{-1}(\tau_t), \qquad
    \tau_t = \frac{c^{u}_t}{c^{u}_t + c^{o}_t}
           = \frac{\pi^{u}_t - \pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t}
                  {\pi^{u}_t - \pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t + \pi^{o}_t},
    \label{eq:optimal_threshold}
\end{equation}
where $F_t$ is the conditional distribution of demand.  When the prices are
unknown, the optimum satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{E}\big[c^u_t\,\mathbb{1}\{y_t > p^*_t\} \,\big|\, \mathcal{I}_t\big]
    = \mathbb{E}\big[c^o_t\,\mathbb{1}\{y_t < p^*_t\} \,\big|\, \mathcal{I}_t\big],
    \label{eq:weighted_quantile}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{I}_t$ is the information available a day ahead: a
\emph{cost-weighted} quantile of demand, in which each outcome is weighted by
the price it would be settled at.  Three consequences motivate the unified
model.  (i)~The target quantile moves with the price, so no model that
targets the mean or a fixed quantile can be optimal.  (ii)~High demand drives
high prices, so shortfalls tend to happen when they are most expensive;
Equation~\eqref{eq:weighted_quantile} accounts for this joint behaviour, a
forecast shifted after training does not.  (iii)~A megawatt-hour of error
during a price spike costs many times more than one at night, so a model
should spend its capacity where prices are high.  A model that predicts the
prices and the demand from shared features and is trained on
Equation~\eqref{eq:excess} with the realised prices can learn all three.

\subsection{Imbalance Prices}
\label{sec:synthetic-prices}

The day-ahead prices in our study are the real Israeli market prices
(Section~\ref{sec:dataset}); imbalance prices are not published for the study
period, so we generate them.  Per 30-minute settlement period,
\begin{gather}
    \pi^{o}_t = \alpha\,(1+\varepsilon_t)\,\pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t, \qquad
    \pi^{u}_t = r_t\,\pi^{o}_t, \\
    r_t = \operatorname{clip}\!\Big(
        \big(r_{\min} + (r_{\max}-r_{\min})\,u_t^{\gamma}\big)\,e^{\eta_t},\;
        r_{\min},\, r_{\max}\Big),
\end{gather}
with $\varepsilon_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_o^2)$,
$\eta_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_u^2)$, and $u_t\in[0,1]$ the position of
$\log\pi^{\mathrm{DA}}_t$ between its 5th and 99th percentiles.  The surplus
price is thus a fixed fraction of the day-ahead price, and the
shortfall-to-surplus ratio lies in $[5,20]$ and rises convexly with
it~\parencite{zhang2022costoriented}.  We use $\alpha=0.3$, $\gamma=2$,
$\sigma_o=0.03$, $\sigma_u=0.15$, $r_{\min}=5$ and $r_{\max}=20$;
$\alpha r_{\min}>1$ guarantees the ordering~\eqref{eq:price-order}.  The
resulting cost-optimal quantile (Figure~\ref{fig:tau-vs-price}) has a median of
$0.78$ on the training set, $0.66$ in the cheapest fifth of the slots and
$0.92$ in the most expensive fifth.
